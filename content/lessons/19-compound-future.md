# Compound future

The future perfect is haber in the future plus the participle. It means "will have done", and it is also a guess about the recent past: *ya habrá llegado* means "he has probably arrived".

The conditional perfect is haber in the conditional plus the participle. It means "would have done".

{{table terminar hacer "Condicional perfecto Indicativo"}}

## Examples

- *Para el viernes habré terminado.* I'll have finished by Friday.
- *¿Dónde habré puesto las llaves?* Where can I have put the keys?
- *Con el coche habríamos llegado antes.* We would have arrived earlier by car.
- *Yo no habría dicho eso.* I wouldn't have said that.
